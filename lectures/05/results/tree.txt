To answer your question, let's look at how parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA and QLoRA work in Lecture 2 (specifically page 52):

* **What LoRA freezes:** The original model weights ($W$), keeping them locked and unmodified during training.
* **What LoRA trains:** Only a small set of additional parameters (represented as low-rank matrices $A$ and $B$) that capture the necessary changes (or delta) for the specific task—often making up less than 1% of the total parameters.
* **What QLoRA adds:** It adds **quantization**, specifically compressing the original frozen model weights down to **4-bit precision**. 

**A helpful way to think about it:** 
Imagine leaving a massive textbook completely untouched (frozen weights), but placing thin sticky notes on specific pages to record your custom notes (the trainable LoRA matrices). QLoRA goes a step further by shrinking the text in the main book into a more compact format (4-bit quantization), which drastically reduces the memory needed so you can train large models on standard GPUs (like a single Colab GPU).
